# The look

OriPass's window is laid out in three panes, in the manner of 1Password for Mac. No artwork, name or sentence of it is used. Everything is drawn from this repo's own code and its own glyphs: SF Symbols and the marks drawn in `Glyphs.swift`. This file says, surface by surface, what is drawn, in what order and in what proportion. Order, presence and proportion are settled here. A figure in points, a delay or anything that moves is a guide to what is built, and `Palette`, `Fonts`, `Metrics` and `Motion` hold the values.

## The window

One window in three columns, with no title drawn anywhere. At the left a sidebar, a shade greyer than the rest, with the traffic lights at its top. A toolbar runs across the top of the other two columns only: back and forward chevrons, then a search field that takes most of the width, with a magnifier and "Search in" followed by the account's email, and at the far right the one filled button in the window, a plus and "New Item" on the accent blue. Under the toolbar sit the item list and, beside it, the details. Hairlines divide the three; nothing else does.

The sidebar is about a quarter of the window, the list a little more than a quarter, and the details the rest.

The search field takes the accent's focus ring and gets a grey round cross at its right end to clear it. What is typed into it takes the item list's place: the header says "Search Results" where the category was and has no sort, one heading counts what was found, and the rows are the list's own, best match first. There is no card under the field. Return chooses the first result and gives the list the keys, the arrows move among the results from the field, and Esc or the cross empties it.

A window too narrow for three columns takes from the details, then from the list, then from the sidebar, and under the three minimums the sidebar folds away; with it folded, the toolbar starts to the right of the traffic lights. A column with nothing in it has a grey sentence in its middle, and the details have the app's mark, faint, over theirs.

## The sidebar

At the top, the account's picture and its email with a chevron at the right, over the plus. It opens a menu: Settings, Sync Now and Lock.

Under it, rows: All Items, Favorites and Password Health; then a section headed VAULTS in small grey capitals, a disclosure chevron before the word and a plus at the row's right end, with Personal first and then the folders (D9), each led by a small round mark in the vault's own colour; then Archive and Recently Deleted. A row is a small mark and a label. The selected row sits on a rounded rectangle nearly as wide as the sidebar, tinted a pale blue-grey. Profile, Developer and Tags are not drawn, because nothing stands behind them (D10). A row that goes nowhere is worse than a row that is not there.

The account's picture is a lettered tile from the email. All Items wears the accent and the star its yellow; the other marks are grey. A vault's mark is a plain dot in its colour. Rows are 34 points tall with 16 between the groups. A collection is drawn after the folders, as one more vault.

The plus beside VAULTS (OP-42) puts a card over a dimmed window, the one the delete question stands on: a folder mark, "New Vault" with a grey sentence under it, a field asking for the name, a line under the field kept for what was refused in red, and Cancel and a blue Create. A right click on a folder's row drops Rename… and Delete…, and Personal and a collection have no menu; the system draws the context menu, so no shot has it. Rename is the same card, with the name in the field and "Rename “name”" over it. Delete is a card with a red bin that says the vault's items are kept and fall back to Personal, with Cancel and a red Delete Vault. A made vault is opened, with VAULTS unfolded if it was shut. VAULTS folded shut hides its rows, the selected one too, and the list stays as it was. Archive is drawn when the vault has something archived.

The foot (OP-43) stands under a hairline like the one under the account: one line shaped as a row and quieter than one, a small grey mark in the column the rows' marks stand in, and under their labels a sentence in the item list's second-line grey, a point smaller than a row. It says "Synced just now", "Synced 2 min ago", then hours and days, "Syncing…" while the server is being asked, and "Offline, showing what this Mac last saw" behind a struck-through Wi-Fi mark when the last time it was asked it did not answer, which at this width takes two lines. It is not a button. The rows scroll above it and it stays.

## The item list

A header row: at the left a small mark of four squares and "All Categories" with a chevron, which is a menu of categories; at the right two glyph buttons, find in this list and sort. The buttons are `text.magnifyingglass` and `arrow.up.arrow.down`, and the sort button stands on a pale rounded square while its menu is open.

Both menus are cards the app draws under their buttons. The sort menu is a grey caption that counts ("7 ITEMS SORTED BY"), then Title, Date Created and Date Modified, a rule, then the two directions, a blue tick at the right of the one in force in each group. The directions under Title are "A to Z" and "Z to A"; under a date they are Newest First and Oldest First. The category menu is All Categories, a rule, and Logins, Secure Notes, Credit Cards, Identities and SSH Keys, each with its mark. A click anywhere else, or Esc, puts a menu away. ⌥⌘F or the find button turns the left of the header into a field, and what is typed there narrows the list to the titles and second lines that hold it; Esc or its cross puts it away and brings the list back.

Rows are grouped under small grey headings in capitals: the month and the year when the list is sorted by a date ("MARCH 2025"), the initial letter when it is sorted by title. A list that has never been sorted is by title, A to Z: a login is looked for by its name. Sorted by title, everything that starts with a digit or a sign is under "#". An item with no date comes last, under "NO DATE". The months are in English.

A row is a rounded-square icon, the title in a medium weight, and under it one grey line. The second line is the username for a login, the brand and last four digits for a card, the full name for an identity, the first line for a note, the network's name for a Wi-Fi item and the fingerprint for an SSH key. A login with no username, a note with nothing in it and a type this app does not know have a title and no second line. A favourite wears a small yellow star over the icon's lower right corner. The selected row sits on a pale grey rounded rectangle inset from the column's edges, 8 points short of each edge. There are no dividers between rows. Rows are 50 points and the icon 32.

The icon is the site's own for a login that has one, in the same rounded square, and a lettered tile on a colour taken from the title until it arrives and for good when there is none or the switch is off. The other four types, and a type this app does not know, wear their category's mark in white on an accent tile. The icons are whatever the icon server hands back.

## Item details

The top row says where the item lives and what can be done to it. At the left the vault, as a menu that moves the item: its mark and name with a chevron. At the right "Edit" with a pencil, and a vertical ellipsis. The count of people with access and its popover are not drawn: a personal Bitwarden item has no people to count, and an organisation's are the Wishlist's (D9). Share is not drawn until there is something behind it.

Then the item's icon, large, with the favourite's star on its corner, and the title beside it in bold at about twice the body size.

Then the main fields inside one rounded outline, each field a row, hairlines between rows. A field is a small label in a violet-leaning blue above its value in body text. For a login the rows are username; password, drawn as dots, with a strength word in grey and a small ring at the row's right end (one of Guessable, Weak, Fair, Good and Strong, with the ring filled a fifth for each and coloured from red to green); and one-time code, drawn as two groups of three digits with a dot between them, followed by a small ring and the seconds left. The ring and the number share a pale grey capsule, and under the pointer the capsule takes the next shade, since the row's fill is its own. A login's first row is called by what is in it: "email" over an address, which is something before one @ and a dotted host after it, and "username" over anything else. Bitwarden keeps the one value either way, and a linked field that points at it says the same word.

A passkey is a row inside the outline, under the password, that says when it was made. Every hidden value is the same ten dots. The one-time code's row is labelled "one-time code", as the form labels the same field, and is the last row of the outline, under a passkey when there is one. Its digits are the body's face a point larger, each as wide as the next so the row does not shift when the code changes; eight digits are two groups of four, and Steam's five letters are one. The ring empties clockwise from the top as the seconds go, and for the last five the ring and the number are the brick red. A secret no code can be made from says "invalid secret" in grey and copies nothing. An item that asks again draws its code as the ten dots until it has been answered for. The secret itself is drawn nowhere outside the form.

Below the outline, fields stand free, label over value with no box: "website" over the address, with the host in link blue and the rest of the URL in the body colour. Bitwarden has no sections, so custom fields form one free-standing group under the websites, in the order the item holds them: a text one as its value, a hidden one as dots, a boolean as a tick or a dash, a linked one as an arrow and the name of the field it points at.

Notes are drawn from Markdown inside their own rounded outline: headings large and bold, bold, italic and strikethrough as written, bulleted lists with round bullets, and a quotation with a short grey bar at its left and grey text.

At the bottom, a disclosure row: a chevron and "Last edited" with the weekday, the date and the time written out. It opens onto one more line, the date the item was created.

Under the title, over the outline, one rounded line stands for each Password Health list the item is on, in a faint red with a red hairline and a warning mark, the sentence in the body, and at the right a grey button. A password seen in a breach, a reused and a weak one say "Edit", unsecured says "Use https", and a card that has run out or will has no button and is as tall as the ones that have. An item on two lists has two lines, the more serious on top (`WatchCard.seriousFirst`). The lines are not drawn while the form is open or before the vault has been counted.

## A field under the pointer

The row fills with a pale grey. The word "Copy" appears at its right in bold, and at the far right a square button with a chevron opens the field's menu. Clicking anywhere on the row copies the value, and the row then says "Copied" where it said "Copy", for a moment.

A password's menu is Reveal and Show in Large Type, and View password history under them only on an item that has an earlier password; Reveal reads Conceal while the row is showing. A row that hides nothing has Show in Large Type alone, and a website has Open in Browser over it. A tick and a pointer at another field do not fill and copy nothing. A note fills nowhere: a "Copy" button stands at its corner while the pointer is over it. A revealed value is set in the fixed-width face, digits and symbols in Large Type's colours.

## Password history

View password history opens a card in the middle of the window over a dimmed one, as the question before a delete stands. "Password history" in the statement face with the item's name under it in grey, a hairline, one row for each earlier password, a hairline and a filled Done at the right. A row is the ten dots over "Last used" and the day in grey, and at its right an eye and a copy glyph. The eye spells the password out in the fixed-width face, digits and symbols in Large Type's two colours, and becomes the slashed eye; the copy glyph is a tick for a moment where "Copied" would be. The rows are as tall as they are and the card with them, up to five and a half, and past that they scroll. An entry the item gives no date for says "Date unknown" and comes after the dated ones. Esc, Done and a click beside the card put it away, and so does choosing another item or opening a form.

## Attachments

An item's files are listed under its custom fields and over the notes, standing free as the fields do. A row is a paperclip in grey, the file's name in body text and its size under it in grey. Under the pointer it is a field's row: the pale fill, "Open" in bold where "Copy" would be, and the square with a chevron, which drops Quick Look and Save As. A click anywhere on the row is Quick Look, in a panel of its own titled as the file is; the red button, Esc and Space put it away, and so do another item and the lock. While the engine is being asked the row says "Fetching…" in grey at its right. What the engine said when it would not hand the file over stands where the size was, in red, until the row is clicked again or another item is chosen. Attach a File is the last row of the item menu's second group. The file on its way in is one more row at the foot, its name over "Adding…", and a file the engine would not take stays there with the engine's sentence in red. The arrows do not stop on these rows yet.

## The item menu

Behind the ellipsis, each row with a glyph at its left: Open in New Window; a rule; Add to Favorites, Move, Duplicate, Attach a File (with a paperclip); a rule; Archive; and Delete in red. Add to Favorites is Remove from Favorites on a favourite. An item in an organisation's collection has no Duplicate row. An archived item has Unarchive where Archive was.

Move opens the menu under the vault's name at the left of the same strip: MOVE TO in small grey capitals, then Personal and the folders, each behind its dot, with a tick on the one the item is in.

An item in Recently Deleted has two rows, Restore and Delete Permanently in red, and the second asks first, on a card over a dimmed window: a red bin, the item's name in the question, the thirty days in the sentence under it, Cancel and a red button. What the engine refused is a sentence on a card with a red outline at the foot of the details, with a cross to dismiss it. A row held over a vault or Archive that would take it puts a ring of the accent around that row. A window of one item is the details column alone under its own traffic lights, with the same strip and the same menus.

## Editing

Edit turns the details into a form in place. Nothing moves: the icon, the title and every field stay where they were and become editable. Cancel and Save stand at the right of the strip, Save faint until something is changed. The title is in an outline of its own.

A login's outlined group is username, password and one-time code, the last two as dots with an eye at the row's right end. The first is called what the details would call what is typed in it, so the label is "username" over "ada" and turns to "email" at the first letter after the dot of "ada@example.test". The password's row keeps its strength, worked out as it is typed, and ends in the wand that raises the generator. A passkey's row stays and cannot be typed into. Each website has its match rule on a small grey button that drops a menu (Default, Base domain, Host, Starts with, Exact, Regular expression, Never) and a circled minus that removes it, with "add a website" under the last. A custom field's label is typed where the label was and its value under it: text, dots, a tick box, or for a linked field a button that says which of username and password it is the same as. At its right an up and a down chevron move it and a circled minus removes it. "add more" drops Text, Hidden, Tick box and Linked. The notes are the Markdown as it is typed. The star on the icon's corner is a button, hollow when the item is no favourite. The row with the caret wears the accent's ring. The two dates are not drawn.

Esc on a form with something changed in it raises a card over the dimmed window, Keep Editing and a red Discard Changes; so does choosing another item. When the item was changed somewhere else, a grey banner over the icon says so, and the form holds the newer copy with what was typed over it.

At the foot of every form, under the notes, stands one row with no outline: "Ask again before this item is used" in body text over a grey line that says what that means, and a switch at the row's right end, the generator's. It sets Bitwarden's master password re-prompt on the item and nothing is sent until Save.

The other four types have the same form with their own outlined group, and no websites. A card's is the cardholder's name, the brand on a grey button that drops Bitwarden's ten and None, the number, the expiry as a month on a button (None, then `01 January` to `12 December`) with the year typed beside it, and the security code. The number and the code are spelled out while the caret is in them and by their eye, and are dots the rest of the time: the number as three groups of them and its last four, the code as ten. A dot the form draws is the size of a bullet in the password's row and as far from the next, so every hidden value in a form is hidden alike; the details' own dots are smaller. An identity's is every one of its eighteen values, empty or not, in the order the details read them, with the short ones two to a row: title and first name, middle and last name, company, email, phone, username, the three lines of the address, postal code and city, state and country, and the three numbers as dots with an eye each. A note has no outlined group, only its custom fields and its notes. A key's is the public key and the fingerprint in grey, which are not typed into, and the private key as ten dots beside a Paste a New Key button; after a paste the two grey rows say the new key's and the third says a new key is kept when the form is saved, and a paste that is no key leaves a red sentence under the group. "add more" leaves Linked out for a note and a key, and for a card and an identity the linked field's menu lists that type's own rows. An item of a type the app does not know has no form.

## New Item

A sheet over the window with five tiles (D11) in one row, Login, Secure Note, Credit Card, Identity and SSH Key, each the category's mark on the accent over its name. The sheet stands a third of the way down the window over a dimmed window, with the field across its top asking "What kind of item?". The first tile is lit. A letter typed leaves the tiles whose name has it, at the left and the size they were, and a word that fits none leaves a grey sentence saying so. ← and → move the lit tile while the field is empty, Tab moves it whatever the field holds, Return takes it, and Esc or a click beside the sheet puts it away.

Choosing a tile closes the sheet and opens the category's empty form in the details column. It is the form Edit opens, empty: the category's mark where the icon will be, "Title" in grey, Save faint until the item has a name, and the vault's name on its menu saying the vault the sidebar had selected, or Personal. A key's form has nothing to type a key into and waits for Paste a New Key, and Save stays faint until one is pasted. Esc on a form with nothing typed puts it away and the details show what they showed. With something typed it asks, "Discard this new item?". The item that was selected stays lit in the list while the form is open.

## The generator

The wand at the right of a login's password row raises a card under it, their right edges together, and stays lit while the card is up. The password stands in a box sunk into the card across the top (`Palette.well`: the field's grey in light, and in dark the content's near-black, a shade under the card) in the fixed-width face, digits and symbols in Large Type's two colours, in a box three lines tall so that a hundred characters fit and the card does not grow as the slider moves. Under the box are the strength, ring and word, and at the right a glyph that asks for another password and one that copies this one, which becomes a tick with "Copied" beside it. Then the three kinds side by side on a track of the same ground, the chosen one raised: Random, Memorable, PIN. Random has Characters, a slider from 8 to 100 with its number, and switches for Numbers and Symbols. Memorable has Words, from 3 to 10, Between, which is five marks side by side (a hyphen, a space drawn as the space bar's mark, a period, a comma, an underscore), and switches for Capitals and A number; the engine has a switch for a number and none for whole words. PIN has Digits, from 4 to 12. Under a hairline, Cancel and a filled Use Password, which puts the password in the form and closes the card; nothing is sent until Save. Esc, the wand again or a click beside the card puts it away, and the password with it.

By itself, from Password Generator in the Item menu, the same card stands over a dimmed window where the New Item sheet stands. It has no field to fill, so its foot is Close and a filled Copy Password, which copies and closes.

## The lock screen

The field sits in the middle of an otherwise empty window under the app's large round mark: the app's pass in white on a disc of the accent, with the account's email under it. The placeholder is "Master password", in a rounded field with the accent's focus ring, and inside the field's right end, after a hairline, a fingerprint glyph in accent blue that asks for the finger. Touch ID is asked for by itself as soon as the lock screen shows, and a wrong password shakes the field. The ground is a pale grey and the field is lighter than it.

A state with nothing to type into (no CLI, nobody signed in, an engine that failed) keeps the mark and puts a sentence, a grey line and one control under it. With nobody signed in the control is the filled "Sign In…" button.

With a pass standing, a grey line at the foot of the window says how many are running. The first unlock after anything was signed through one has a card along the foot of the panes, one line and OK, which the lock also takes.

Signing in is a card over a scrim, as the Touch ID question is, left-aligned and about a third of the window wide. The form: a title, one grey line that says the password is not kept, a track of three segments for the server (bitwarden.com, bitwarden.eu, Self-hosted, the chosen one lifted off the track, and an address field under it for the third), the email, the master password, and a row with "Use an API key" in the accent at the left and a grey Cancel and the filled Sign In at the right. The API key turns the two fields into `client_id` and `client_secret` and the grey line into where the key is found. A refusal is one red sentence between the fields and the buttons. While it works the fields dim and a small spinner sits left of Cancel. The second step replaces the form inside the same card: either a list of the account's methods as grey rows with a chevron, or one field for a code under a line that says where the code comes from, with Back and Continue.

The Touch ID sheet is the system's own: the fingerprint with the app's icon on its corner, the app's name in bold, one sentence that says the app is trying to unlock, a second that offers the finger or the account's password, a filled default button for the password and a grey Cancel. The reason is "unlock OriPass", and the button says what the system says when nothing is passed.

Touch ID is first offered on a card in the middle of the window that has just opened, over a scrim: the fingerprint in the accent, a question in the statement's weight, three grey lines that say the password is not kept and that fourteen days ends it, and two buttons side by side, a grey "Not Now" and the filled "Use Touch ID". Return takes the filled one and Escape the other.

An item that asks again is answered for with the system's Touch ID sheet when a finger can open the vault, under a sentence that says which of five things the app is trying to do and names no item. When no finger can, a card stands in the middle of the screen in a panel of its own, over every window, built as the Touch ID question is: a lock in the accent, the item's name in the statement ("“GitHub” asks for the master password"), one grey sentence that says before what and that the password is not kept, the field on the card's sunk ground with the accent's ring, a red sentence under it when the engine did not take the password, and a grey Cancel beside a filled Continue. Return is Continue, and Esc, Cancel and a click on another window are a no. Nothing in the details marks an item that asks.

## Quick Access

A wide rounded panel, a light grey with a little transparency in light mode, floating over whatever is in front. At the top a large search field, lighter than the panel and with no ring, an accent magnifier at its left and the typed text large. The panel's right end has no picture.

Over a chosen item it shows the item as a header: its icon, its title in bold, a grey line of the username and the address run together and cut off with an ellipsis, and a close cross at the right. A rule. Then actions, one a row, the name at the left and its key combination at the right in grey. An opened item's actions are Copy Username, which is Copy Email for a login whose username is an address, Copy Password and, for a login that has one, Copy One-Time Code, with their keys; Open in New Window and Open in OriPass; and then every remaining field of the item as its own copy row, an address named by its host when there are several. The chosen action is filled with the accent. With an app to type into, "Fill in" and that app's name stands between the two windows with ⇧Return beside it; a panel that came up over OriPass itself has no such row. "Open & Fill" with ⌥Return beside it stands before that row, for a login with an address a browser opens and a password; the panel goes and the browser comes forward. A footer strip, a shade darker, says "Located in" and names the vault with its dot. The left arrow goes back from the actions.

The results are each one line, with a small icon, the title in bold and the username and address in grey after a dash, cut off with an ellipsis. The grey after the dash is the line the item list gives the item and then the site's host, not the whole address. At most eight rows show at a time, and the arrows move the eight down a longer list. The selected row is filled accent blue with white text and shows the action Return will take at its right, which is "Copy Password", or the name of the type's first field for an item with none, and it has a small grey square with a vertical ellipsis beside it that opens the actions. Under the list a strip of key hints, each combination in keycaps with its action after it: ⌘C, ⇧⌘C and → with what each does to the selected row.

Before a letter the list is led by the logins for what is in front, under a small grey name, the site's when a browser said which page it is on and otherwise the app's, and then the items used last under "Recently Used"; with nothing to suggest the used are listed with no name over them. With nothing used either the panel is the field alone, 600 points wide, the field 42 high and 8 in from each edge, the word "Search" where nothing is typed, its top edge a third of the way down the screen the pointer is on. Over a shut vault the row under the field is the lock: the mark, the master password's field, and the fingerprint when a finger can open it. The panel grows downwards and does not change size with ⌘+ and ⌘-.

When a fill was asked for and macOS has not yet allowed the typing, the list gives way to a notice at the lock's height and a half: a round accent mark with a keyboard in it, "Password copied, not typed" in bold over one grey sentence saying what Accessibility is for, and at the right a filled button, "Allow Typing…", which Return presses. The same notice with no button, "Nothing typed" over its one sentence, is what the panel comes up with when a login with no username was to be typed and the caret was in no password's field; Return puts it away.

## The menu under a field

A rounded white card a few points under the field, with a hairline edge and a soft shadow. Each result is one line: a small tile, the title in bold, then a dash and the username in grey, cut off with an ellipsis. The chosen one is filled with the accent and says at its right end what Return will do, which is "Fill". Under a rule, a strip of key hints.

A mark stands in the field, 16 points, its right edge 6 in from the field's and in the blue of `Palette.lit`, on any field a login could go in. A click on it, a click in a field with a row to offer or ↓ in the field opens the card 4 under the field, as wide as the field but no narrower than 280 and no wider than 400, and over the field if it would go past the foot of the window. The rows are 32 high, six before the list scrolls, in a card with 6 of padding and the corners of `Metrics.groupRadius`, on `menu`, with `outline` for its edge and `shadow` under it, and `lit` for the chosen row. An item's icon would have to ask the app for more than a title, so a row has a tile with the title's first letter, in `lit` and the same for every row, white on the chosen one. The strip under a rule has ↑↓ Choose, ↩ Fill and esc Close, in grey with the keys a shade fainter and no keycaps, since the card is small and the keys are the same on every Mac. A card with no rows is one grey line saying why: nothing for this site, the app locked or not open, this browser not allowed. A click in the field says nothing of that, and only the mark and ↓, which asked, are told. The field keeps the keys the whole time, so the card can be typed past: Tab closes it, a press outside closes it. The popup behind the toolbar's button is the same list and the same lines on the same ground, 340 wide, with no edge of its own, and says "Waiting for OriPass…" until the app has answered. It arrives as Quick Access's rows do on `Motion.quick`, rising 4 points, and under Reduce Motion it only fades.

On a site where the vault has a passkey, a key 12 wide stands 2 to the left of the mark, stroked in the same blue with nothing behind it, because a field's ground is the page's and could be any colour. The card then has a grey line over the strip saying that the passkey is offered when the site asks. It says that because a page that has not asked has nothing to sign. In `docs/toast/`, `mark-passkey`.

In a page's top frame the mark stands alone. The browser's own key and its Strong Password words are hidden and their room goes back to the text, by `Extension/page.css`. A frame keeps them, since a frame of another site has no mark, and the contacts button stays everywhere.

A field with no login for the site offers what the vault already uses. A small grey heading, "Used in your vault", stands over the rows, 11 points on a line of 16. Each row is one word, an address or a username, in the title's weight with nothing after it, most used first. Its tile is on `cell` with a grey letter, or a grey @ for an address, so that it does not pass for one of the site's logins, whose tile is `lit`. An email field lists addresses alone. The chosen row says "Fill", as a login's does, and a click on it puts that word in that field and no other. A click in the field opens this card only while the field is empty, so that it never comes up over something typed, and the mark and ↓ open it either way. In `docs/toast/`, `field-words`.

A password field in a sign-up has one row, "Make a strong password", chosen from the start, so its tile is white with a wand and two sparks stroked in `lit`, and under it a grey sentence saying that it goes into each new password field and that OriPass asks to save it once the site takes it. A click in the field opens this as well. The row asks for nothing until it is chosen, and the card then says "Waiting for OriPass…" until the engine has answered. Where a form has one new password and nothing else, this row leads the site's logins, because sites write `new-password` on a sign-in to keep a browser out. ## Colour and type

Light mode: white content; near-black text and a mid grey for second lines; one accent blue for the New Item button, links, ticks and the focus ring; and a second, more violet blue for field labels. The red is a brick red, used for Delete and for a Password Health card that needs attention; the star is a warm yellow. The sidebar is a material and not a flat colour, a warm light grey that takes a tint from the desktop behind it. Its selected row is a darker blue-grey of the same family. The list's selected row is a pale neutral grey, and the Quick Access panel is a light grey of its own.

Dark mode: the content is a neutral near-black with no blue in it, about 28 28 28. The sidebar is not neutral: it is a dark slate with a clear blue in it, about 32 42 56, and its selected row is a saturated mid blue, about 36 62 104, which is much more colour than a grey selection would have. The hairline between the two is nearly black, darker than either, and not light. Text is white, second lines and the small capital headings are a mid grey, the search field is a dark rounded field with a thin lighter edge, a shade under the content, and the star, the four-square mark beside "All Categories" and the accent stay as bright as in light mode. Since OP-30 the content, the menus and the fields are neutral greys, the sidebar is a slate, its selected row a blue, and the line between two columns is black where every other line is a faint white.

Every word is at least 4.5 to 1 against what it is drawn on, in both appearances, which `make contrast` works out from `Palette.readings` (OP-57). The second lines are a darker grey in light and a lighter one in dark, so that they read on a selected row and on Quick Access's grey. A placeholder is its own grey, `prompt`, between the second line's and the faint one, and the faint grey is left to glyphs and never draws a word. White stands on `lit` and not on the accent: the same blue in light, and in dark a deeper one, since the dark accent that links are drawn in is too light to carry white. So in dark the New Item button, a lit row of a menu, Quick Access's selected row, a category's tile and the disc of the mark are one deep blue and a link is a lighter one. The button that deletes for good has a deeper red in dark for the same reason. A lettered tile has a deeper shade of its vault's colour, the same in light and in dark, so the amber is an ochre and the green a bottle green; a vault's dot keeps the brighter one. Nothing drawn at half strength because it cannot be used is counted, and a row of the settings that is off keeps the grey line that says why at full strength.

Compact is the Appearance room's second density. A row is about five sixths of its height (a row of the list 42 points for 50, a field 40 for 48, a sidebar row 29 for 34) and a padding of twelve points or more is three quarters of itself. No face, icon, control or corner changes, so compact is the same type with less air, and about a sixth more rows of the list in the same window. Quick Access, Large Type and the mark's menu are built to their own measures and do not follow it.

The type is SF. Every value lives in `Palette`, `Fonts` and `Metrics`; the numbers above are a guide to hue and weight, and those files are the source.
